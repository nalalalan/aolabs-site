Quantifying the Effect of Non-Physical Parameters on the Nonlinear Dynamics of an Electromechanical Ratcheting Mechanism

Shunsuke Winston, Alan Pham, Brennan Bahr, Aabhas Singh, Chris Schumann, Rob Flicek, Scott Grutzik, Robert J. Kuether, and Kumar Vemaganti.

Research record

Summer 2025 internship work on a watch-like ratcheting mechanism, focused on separating physical response from sensitivity introduced by finite-element setup choices.

The study evaluated submodel behavior under shock and vibration while varying mesh density, momentum-balance iteration, and processor count. The public record includes the coauthored paper, the final NOMAD presentation, Alan's Soft Robotics Lab presentation, the completed slide-81 result matrix, and extracted FEA media from the final deck.

NOMAD internship context

NOMAD stands for Nonlinear Mechanics and Dynamics Research Institute. Alan's 2025 internship ran June 16-August 8 in Albuquerque through Sandia National Laboratories and the University of New Mexico.

The institute placed interns in Sandia research groups for in-person finite-element and nonlinear dynamics work. Alan's Soft Robotics Lab talk documented the research setting, weekly mentor cadence, project-team structure, and the link between this work and soft robotics/compliant mechanism modeling.

Study structure

Component Scope Reason for isolation
Pin-spring-pawl Contact-free spring/pin interaction Baseline convergence behavior without the dominant contact/friction interfaces.
Pin-pawl Pin interface with pawl body Tests sensitivity where local contact response begins to dominate the model.
Pawl-gear Ratcheting gear contact Separates gear-to-pawl contact behavior under shock and sinusoidal vibration.

FEA study results

The completed summary matrix on slide 81 classifies each submodel by whether the response converged as non-physical parameters were varied.

Pin-spring-pawl converged across momentum-balance iteration, mesh density, and processor-count sweeps. Pin-pawl did not converge across the same sweeps. Pawl-gear converged for processor count, but remained sensitive for momentum-balance iteration and mesh density.

Parameter varied Pin-spring-pawl Pin-pawl Pawl-gear
Momentum-balance iteration Converged Non-convergent / chaotic Non-convergent / chaotic
Mesh density Converged Non-convergent / chaotic Non-convergent / chaotic
Processor count Converged Non-convergent / chaotic Converged

Result notes by submodel

Pin-spring-pawl

Complete convergence between runs for the momentum-balance, mesh-density, and processor-count sweeps. The Haversine cases showed that one momentum-balance iteration was noisier but still tracked the trend; the sinusoidal cases converged while the idealized model underpredicted force.

Pin-pawl

Rotation and contact force became erratic after contact/friction dominated. Very coarse mesh cases produced lock-up behavior, and sinusoidal vibration amplified divergence relative to the Haversine shock cases.

Pawl-gear

Momentum-balance iteration and mesh density remained sensitive. Processor-count variation was the one Pawl-gear parameter class marked convergent in the completed slide-81 summary.
