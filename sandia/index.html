Quantifying model-setup sensitivity in an electromechanical ratcheting mechanism

Shunsuke Winston, Alan Pham, Brennan Bahr, Aabhas Singh, Chris Schumann, Rob Flicek, Scott Grutzik, Robert J. Kuether, and Kumar Vemaganti.

Open PDF Paper page

Research record

Summer 2025 internship work on a watch-like ratcheting mechanism, focused on separating physical response from sensitivity introduced by finite-element setup choices.

The study evaluated submodel behavior under shock and vibration while varying mesh density, momentum-balance iteration, and processor count. The public record includes the coauthored paper, the final NOMAD presentation, Alan's Soft Robotics Lab presentation, and extracted mechanism media from the final deck.

Study structure

Component Scope Reason for isolation
Pin-spring-pawl Contact-free spring/pin interaction Baseline convergence behavior without the dominant contact/friction interfaces.
Pin-pawl Pin interface with pawl body Tests sensitivity where local contact response begins to dominate the model.
Pawl-gear Ratcheting gear contact Separates gear-to-pawl contact behavior under shock and sinusoidal vibration.

Result boundary

The pin-spring-pawl model was comparatively insensitive to the parameter sweeps. Pin-pawl and pawl-gear cases showed stronger sensitivity where contact and friction dominated, especially in the vibration cases.

The paper frames the conclusion as a submodel-level study and points to future assembly-level work rather than claiming a complete mechanism validation.
